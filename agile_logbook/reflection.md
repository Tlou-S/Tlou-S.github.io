# Agile Logbook — Reflection

[← Back to portfolio](../) · [View Evidence](evidence.html)

## What I produced

An Agile logbook that covers August to the present. It has a taskboard, a walkthrough of the board, stand-up entries grouped by sprint, and retrospective notes. The entries follow my work from the EduInsight AI project, through my university group projects, to the Soluon YES app.

## What I learned

Keeping a log turned vague feelings such as "I'm stuck" into specific impediments I could raise: unsure of the next step, on the wrong branch, waiting to push. Once these were written down, they were easy to take to Shaun and fix. The taskboard also showed me my whole workload at a glance, including what is finished, what is in progress and what belongs to Inam on the Youth side. The retrospective notes helped me see a pattern: I lose time at the start of a new piece of work when I do not confirm the basics first, so I now check the branch, the scope and the push rules on day one.

## Challenges

I moved between projects with different deadlines, so some of my earlier entries were written afterwards from my notes and have less detail. Work I have not yet pushed also meant the logbook was the only record of some progress, which is a risk if it is not kept up to date.

## What I would do differently

I would write a short entry at the end of every day, include the branch or commit where relevant, and use each retrospective to choose one improvement and check it at the next sprint.

## Connecting the five sections

The charter set how we work, the user stories defined what to build, the product backlog ranked it, the sprint backlog scheduled it across August to October, and the logbook recorded what actually happened. Using the same US-IDs throughout let me trace one feature, such as host login, from idea to approval to implementation.
