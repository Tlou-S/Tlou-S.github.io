# Sprint Backlog — Reflection

[← Back to portfolio](../) · [View Evidence](evidence.html)

## What I produced

A sprint backlog that runs from August to the present in five sprints. Each sprint has a goal, the backlog items and tasks I pulled in, and an outcome. The sprints follow how my work changed: the EduInsight AI project in the WIL programme, my university group projects (UniTrade and AIS1A), and then the Soluon YES app, where I work on the Host/SME side.

## What I learned

A sprint goal keeps a block of work focused, even when the work is very different from one sprint to the next. In the EduInsight sprint the goal was role-based login with a lecturer approval workflow. In the Soluon sprint it was a working host flow for the hackathon. Choosing only the items that served the goal meant I left other ideas, such as AI matching, for a later sprint. I also learned that the sprint backlog belongs to me as the developer: I decide how to break the work into tasks, while Shaun decides the priority.

## Challenges

My sprints are longer than a week because I moved between projects with different deadlines, so they do not follow one fixed rhythm. The Soluon sprint started slowly because I was unsure of the next step after cloning the repo and began on the wrong branch. Changes I have not yet pushed also make progress harder to show, because the team can only see what is on the shared branch.

## What I would do differently

I would plan fewer items per sprint with clearer acceptance criteria, and keep a short log during each sprint instead of rebuilding details at the end. At Soluon I would confirm the branch and push rules on day one, and push small changes more often once Shaun agrees.
